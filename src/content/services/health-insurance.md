## How health insurance works

A health plan shares the cost of your medical care with you. You pay a monthly **premium** to keep your coverage active. When you get care, you may first pay a **deductible** — the amount you pay before the plan starts sharing costs — and then **copays** or **coinsurance** for each visit or service.

Every plan also has an **out-of-pocket maximum**. Once your spending reaches it, the plan pays 100% of covered in-network care for the rest of the year. Many preventive services, such as yearly checkups and certain screenings, are covered without cost-sharing when you use in-network providers.

## Comparing plans

Individual plans sold through the marketplace are grouped into **metal levels** — Bronze, Silver, Gold and Platinum. The levels describe how costs are split between you and the plan, not the quality of care. Bronze plans usually have lower premiums but higher costs when you need care, while Gold and Platinum plans cost more each month but less at the doctor’s office.

Networks matter as much as price. Before choosing, it is worth confirming:

- Your **doctors and hospitals** are in the plan’s network.
- Your **prescriptions** are on the plan’s drug list, and what they will cost.
- How the plan handles **referrals** and specialist visits.
- What you would pay in a **realistic year** of care — not just the monthly premium.

## When you can enroll

In New Jersey, individuals and families can buy coverage through **Get Covered New Jersey**, the state’s official marketplace, during the yearly open enrollment period, which generally runs from November 1 through January 31. Outside that window, a major life event — such as losing job-based coverage, getting married, having or adopting a child, or moving — may open a special enrollment period for a limited time after the event.

New Jersey also requires most residents to have health coverage, or to pay a shared responsibility payment when they file their state income taxes, unless they qualify for an exemption.

## Things to consider

- **Look beyond the premium.** A low monthly cost can mean higher bills when you need care.
- **Report income changes.** If you receive financial help, updating your income during the year helps you avoid surprises at tax time.
- **Check the network every year.** Doctors and drug lists can change when your plan renews.
- **Turning 65 soon?** Medicare has its own enrollment rules and deadlines — our Medicare 101 guide explains them step by step.
