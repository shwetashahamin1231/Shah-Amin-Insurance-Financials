## How an annuity works

An annuity is a contract between you and an insurance company. You pay in — either a single lump sum or a series of payments — and in return the company agrees to grow your money, pay you income, or both, according to the terms of the contract.

Most annuities have two phases. During the **accumulation phase**, your money grows tax-deferred. During the **income phase**, you can turn the value into regular payments, take withdrawals as you need them, or leave it to grow for your heirs. Some annuities skip straight to income.

## The main types

- **Fixed annuities** credit a stated interest rate for a set period. They are similar in spirit to a certificate of deposit, but with tax deferral and the backing of an insurance company rather than FDIC insurance.
- **Fixed indexed annuities** credit interest based partly on how a market index performs. Your gains are limited by caps, participation rates or spreads, but your principal is not reduced when the index falls.
- **Immediate income annuities** turn a lump sum into payments that begin right away and can last for your lifetime, for a set number of years, or for the lifetimes of two people.
- **Deferred income annuities** work the same way, but payments begin on a future date you choose.

## Income for life

One of the most valued features of an annuity is the option to receive income you cannot outlive. Some contracts build this in; others offer it through an optional rider, usually for an added cost.

For many retirees, covering essential bills — housing, food, insurance — with dependable income alongside Social Security brings real peace of mind. It can also make the rest of your savings easier to manage, because day-to-day market ups and downs matter less when the basics are already taken care of.

## Things to consider

- **Annuities are long-term contracts.** Plan to leave the money in place through the surrender period.
- **Keep an emergency fund elsewhere.** Use savings you will not need on short notice.
- **Understand every rider and fee.** Optional benefits can add cost, so make sure each one earns its place.
- **Guarantees come from the insurer.** They are backed by the financial strength and claims-paying ability of the issuing company, so its ratings matter.
- **Annuities are not bank deposits.** They are not FDIC-insured.
