## What life insurance does

At its simplest, life insurance pays a sum of money — the death benefit — to the people you name when you pass away. That money can replace your income, pay off a mortgage, cover college costs, settle final expenses or leave a legacy. In most cases the benefit is paid to your beneficiaries free of federal income tax, so it can go a long way at a difficult time.

Life insurance is not only for parents of young children. Couples who rely on two incomes, business owners, retirees who want to leave something behind, and people caring for an aging parent or a child with special needs all use it — each for their own reasons.

## Term and permanent: the two main paths

**Term life insurance** covers you for a set period, such as 10, 20 or 30 years. If you pass away during the term, your beneficiaries receive the benefit. Term usually costs the least when you first buy it, which makes it a practical way to protect your family during the years they depend on you most. Many term policies can be converted to permanent coverage later without new health questions, within the limits the policy sets.

**Permanent life insurance** is designed to last your whole life, as long as premiums are paid. The main kinds are:

- **Whole life**, with level premiums and cash value that grows at a rate set by the policy.
- **Universal life**, which offers more flexibility in how much you pay and how much coverage you keep.
- **Indexed universal life**, where cash value growth is linked to a market index, with limits on both gains and losses.

Cash value builds gradually over time, and you may be able to borrow against it. Loans and withdrawals reduce the death benefit and can have tax consequences, so they deserve careful planning.

## Choosing an amount

There is no single formula. We start with a few simple questions. Who would need financial support, and for how long? What debts would remain? Which future goals — college, a paid-off home, a gift to your place of worship or a favorite charity — do you want to protect? What savings and coverage do you already have?

From there, we suggest a sensible range and show you the cost of each option side by side. You can then choose the balance of protection and budget that feels right for your family.

## Things to consider

- **Your age and health matter.** Premiums are generally based on your age and health when you apply, so starting earlier often costs less.
- **Keep beneficiaries up to date.** Review them after a marriage, divorce, birth or death in the family.
- **Read the riders.** Options such as waiver of premium or accelerated benefits for serious illness can add valuable protection.
- **Don’t cancel old coverage too soon.** Keep an existing policy in force until a new one has been approved and is active.
